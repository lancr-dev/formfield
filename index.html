The idea / 01

A whole story.
In good company.

Think of a bento box. Different compartments, each with a purpose, coming together as one considered whole.

On a website, those compartments hold your work, your products, your personality, and your next big idea. Different-sized tiles create a natural reading path, so visitors can get the picture quickly and explore what catches their eye.

It’s a strong fit when your brand has several things to say. The craft is deciding what deserves the spotlight and what belongs beside it.

A few good questions

Curious?
Good.

Here are a few things to know before you choose a direction.

Is bento right for every website? +

It works especially well when you have a mix of content to explore: projects, products, services, or brand stories. Long articles and step-by-step tasks usually benefit from a simpler reading flow. A custom site can use both.

What happens to the grid on a phone? +

The composition changes. Wide tiles become full-width sections, supporting tiles move into a natural reading order, and text gets the space it needs. Mobile should feel intentionally designed, rather than like a smaller desktop page.

Can it use my existing colors and identity? +

Yes. Bento is a way of arranging content, so it can take on your typography, colors, imagery, and tone. The concepts here are starting points for a conversation about your own identity.

Does a bento website need lots of animation? +

No. The layout should communicate clearly while standing still. Interactions can help people explore or confirm an action, and motion should respect visitors who prefer a quieter experience.

What should I prepare for a custom website? +

Start with your goal, intended audience, key pages, and any copy or brand assets you already have. A preferred timeline and budget range also help define the scope. You can use the brief builder above to capture a first direction.
